\begin{abstract}
A prior probability for a binary outcome often exists already, and a recurrent network trained freely on each
example's history can end up worse than that prior. We anchor a gated recurrent network on the prior's log-odds,
let it add only a bounded offset whose head starts at zero, and penalise the squared offset in output space with a
strength chosen on an inner holdout. On a known-answer synthetic task with \nSizeM{} training rows, held-out
log-loss falls by \nSynMDabsPrior{} nats (SE \nSynMSEthreePrior) against the prior alone, by \nSynMDabsLzero{}
(SE \nSynMSEthreeLzero) against the same anchored model without the penalty and by \nSynMDabsUnanch{}
(SE \nSynMSEthreeUnanch) against an unanchored recurrent network, the only setting where it beats every arm by more
than \nKeepSE{} SE. On ICU mortality it beats the prior and both recurrent arms but loses to gradient-boosted trees
started from the prior by \nPhysMMDabsGbm{} nats, and under a strong prior it ties the prior while the unpenalised
model is \nPhysGbmMMDabsLzero{} nats worse. All differences are paired on fixed held-out sets of
\nPhysMMNtest{} to \nElecMMNtest{} rows, pooled over \nSplitCount{} training subsamples, with per-example
standard errors (a moving-block bootstrap for the time series).
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem}
\label{sec:problem}

Many binary forecasting problems come with a prior probability $q_i$ before any model is trained: a clinical
severity score, an upstream model, a probability quoted by someone else. A sequence model that reads the example's
recent multichannel history can add what the prior misses. Trained freely, it can also do worse than the prior. It
has to relearn what the prior already knows, it overfits at small $n$, and nothing ties it to the prior when the
data shift between training and test.

Our runs show both failures. On ICU mortality with \nSizeD{} training stays, a recurrent network that gets the
prior as an input reaches a held-out log-loss of \nPhysDUnanchLL{} nats against \nPhysDPriorLL{} for the prior
alone. The usual fix is a residual: start from the prior's log-odds and learn a correction, as in residual policy
learning \citep{silver2018residual,johannink2019residual} and residual physics \citep{zeng2020tossingbot}. That fix
is not enough on its own. The same anchored residual without a penalty reaches \nPhysDLzeroLL{}, still worse than
the prior. Weight decay does not bound it either: Adam rescales each update by the gradient's own size, so small
gradients still move the head far. With decay \nNetWd{} on every weight, the unpenalised head sits at its bound on
\nPhysDLzeroAtCap{}\% of the training rows.

The goal is a correction that stays close to the prior unless the data say otherwise, and that still uses the
sequence when it carries signal.

\section{The change}
\label{sec:change}

The model keeps the prior as an anchor on the log-odds scale and adds a bounded, gated offset from a gated recurrent
unit (GRU, a standard recurrent cell) \citep{cho2014gru}:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:model}
b_i = \operatorname{logit} q_i, \qquad
r_i = c \tanh\!\left(w^{\top} h_{i,T} + \beta\right) v_i, \qquad
p_i = \sigma\!\left(b_i + r_i\right),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:loss}
\mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} \operatorname{BCE}\!\left(y_i, p_i\right)
  + \lambda \, \frac{\sum_{i} r_i^{2}}{\sum_{i} v_i},
\qquad \operatorname{BCE}(y,p) = -y\log p - (1-y)\log(1-p).
\end{equation}
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{\hskip 1.5em}ll@{}}
\toprule
Symbol & Meaning & Symbol & Meaning \\
\midrule
$q_i$, $b_i$ & prior probability, its log-odds (clipped) & $v_i$ & validity gate, 0 or 1 \\
$h_{i,T}$ & GRU state after the last step & $c$ & bound on the offset (the cap) \\
$w$, $\beta$ & head weights and bias, start at 0 & $\lambda$ & penalty strength \\
$r_i$ & offset added to the prior's log-odds & $\sigma$ & logistic function \\
$p_i$ & predicted probability & $y_i$ & observed label, 0 or 1 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The GRU reads each value channel relative to its last observed value after a forward fill, plus one 0/1
observation-mask channel per value channel, the mask of GRU-D \citep{che2018grud} without its decay. An example
whose observed share of steps is below \nMinObs{} gets $v_i = 0$ and therefore returns the prior. The head starts
at zero, so the untrained model equals the prior exactly; this zero start is the ReZero idea
\citep{bachlechner2021rezero} applied to a probability. With $\lambda = 0$ the loss is the plain cross-entropy
tensor and training matches an unpenalised loop bit for bit, which the package tests.

The new part is the penalty. It acts on each example's offset $r_i$, not on the weights as weight decay or L2-SP
\citep{li2018l2sp} do. Read as a maximum a posteriori fit (the most probable parameters), $\lambda r_i^2$ is a
zero-mean Gaussian prior on the correction itself. The cap bounds the worst case, and the penalty sets how much
evidence a correction needs. The strength $\lambda$ is picked from \nSweepCount{} values on an inner
\nInnerPct\% holdout of the training rows, with ties going to the larger value. The lemma below states what the
penalty does at the population level; the proof is in Appendix~\ref{app:proof}.

\begin{lemma}[pointwise shrinkage]
\label{lem:shrink}
Fix one input with anchor $b$ and let $\mu = \Pr(y = 1 \mid \text{input}) \in (0,1)$. Let
$\lambda' = \lambda n / \sum_i v_i > 0$. The objective $f(r) = \mathbb{E}[\operatorname{BCE}(y, \sigma(b + r))] +
\lambda' r^2$ is strictly convex, and its minimiser $r^{*}$ solves $\sigma(b + r^{*}) + 2\lambda' r^{*} = \mu$.
(i) $r^{*} = 0$ if and only if the prior is calibrated at that input, $\sigma(b) = \mu$.
(ii) $r^{*}$ has the sign of $\operatorname{logit}\mu - b$.
(iii) $|r^{*}| < |\operatorname{logit}\mu - b|$ whenever $\sigma(b) \neq \mu$.
With the cap, the offset is also below $c$ in size.
\end{lemma}

In words: the penalised correction moves toward the calibrated one and always stops short of it. A calibrated
prior is left alone. The lemma is a statement about the population. It is not a guarantee that a fitted model on a
finite sample never does worse than its prior.

\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Data.} Three public tasks, each with a held-out test set that no training, tuning or penalty choice
touches. \emph{Synthetic}: a logistic prior on static features, \nSynChannels{} random-walk channels of
\nSynSteps{} steps with \nSynMissPct\% of values missing, and a known true probability, so the best possible
log-loss is known (\nSynBayesLL{} nats); \nSynMNtest{} test rows. \emph{elec2} \citep{harries1999elec2,gama2004drift}:
does the electricity price end above its daily average; the prior is a logistic regression on the current row,
refitted each week on the previous \nElecPriorRows{} rows; the sequence is the last \nElecWindow{} half-hours of
\nElecCols{} columns; \nSplitCount{} forward-chaining folds give \nElecMMNtest{} test rows. \emph{PhysioNet 2012}
\citep{silva2012physionet,goldberger2000physiobank}: in-hospital mortality from an hourly grid of \nPhysVars{}
variables over \nPhysHours{} hours ($\nPhysPosRate\%$ positive); the prior is a logistic regression on the SAPS-I
admission score \citep{legall1984saps}, fitted out of fold so the residual does not learn to undo the prior's own
overfit; \nPhysMMNtest{} test stays. Training sizes run from \nSizeCCL{} to \nSizeMMMM{} rows.

\paragraph{Arms.} Every arm sees the same windows, rows and seed. \emph{Prior alone}. \emph{Penalty 0}: the
anchored model of Eq.~\eqref{eq:model} with $\lambda = 0$. \emph{Unanchored GRU}: the same network started at the
base rate, without cap or penalty, with the prior's log-odds as an extra input channel. \emph{LightGBM from prior}:
gradient-boosted trees \citep{ke2017lightgbm} whose starting score is the prior's log-odds, with early stopping on
the same inner holdout, on per-variable summaries (PhysioNet) or the network's own flattened steps. \emph{Ours}:
the anchored model with $\lambda$ chosen as in Section~\ref{sec:change}. The fixed-$\lambda$ sweep and
\nAblationCount{} ablations run alongside. The network is small and identical across arms: hidden size
\nNetHidden, cap $c = \nNetCap$, AdamW \citep{loshchilov2019adamw} with learning rate $\nNetLr$ and weight decay
\nNetWd, one seed and one bag per arm (Appendix~\ref{app:settings}).

\paragraph{Metric and error.} The metric is held-out log-loss in nats, $\ell_i = \operatorname{BCE}(y_i, p_i)$.
For each arm $a$ we report the paired difference
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:diff}
\bar d = \frac{1}{n_{\text{test}}} \sum_{i} \left(\ell_i^{\text{ours}} - \ell_i^{a}\right),
\qquad \operatorname{SE} = \frac{s_d}{\sqrt{n_{\text{test}}}},
\end{equation}
where $s_d$ is the standard deviation of the per-example differences, after averaging each test example over the
\nSplitCount{} training subsamples. Negative means ours is better. For elec2 the rows are serially dependent, so the
SE is a moving-block bootstrap \citep{kunsch1989bootstrap} with one-week blocks of \nElecBlock{} rows and
\nBootDraws{} draws. A difference counts as a win only beyond \nKeepSE{} SE and as a tie inside one SE.

\section{Limits}
\label{sec:limits}

The win over every arm holds in one setting. On PhysioNet the method loses to boosted trees started from the prior,
and the \nVariantCount{} variants we ran to close that gap did not close it (Appendix~\ref{app:extra}): a prior
blind to the second day, a boosted-tree prior, and ten times the weight decay. The last one helps ours
(\nPhysWdMMOursLL{} at \nSizeMM) but helps the unanchored GRU more (\nPhysWdMMUnanchLL); a cap and penalty hold the
model near a weak anchor. These variants were chosen after seeing the PhysioNet result and are post hoc. The arms,
the \nSweepCount-value sweep, the \nAblationCount{} ablations, the training sizes and the keep rule were fixed
before any run. One correction was made after a run: the first elec2 tree arm saw a shorter lag window
(log-loss \nElecOldGbmLL); the table uses the rerun on the network's own steps.

The SE covers test-sample noise only. Over the \nSplitCount{} training subsamples the SE of synthetic
$n = \nSizeM$ against penalty 0 is \nSynMSEsplitLzero{} instead of \nSynMSELzero, which puts that win at the
\nKeepSE{} SE boundary. Each arm is one network, not the package default of \nDefSeeds{} seeds times \nDefBags{}
bags, and full-size training, a GRU-D arm and a second real time-series task were cut for compute. We do not claim
a finite-sample no-harm guarantee, an edge over boosted trees on tabular summaries, or novelty for the zero start or
the cap.

\section{Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:shrink}}
\label{app:proof}

\textbf{Given.}
1) $\sigma(z) = 1/(1 + e^{-z})$ has $\sigma'(z) = \sigma(z)(1 - \sigma(z)) > 0$ for every real $z$.
2) $\frac{d}{dz}\log\sigma(z) = 1 - \sigma(z)$ and $\frac{d}{dz}\log(1 - \sigma(z)) = -\sigma(z)$.
3) A differentiable function with a strictly increasing derivative is strictly convex, and a point where that
derivative is zero is its unique minimiser.
4) Intermediate value theorem: a continuous $g$ with $g(a) < 0 < g(b)$ has a root strictly between $a$ and $b$.

\textbf{Choose:} $z = b + r$, and per example the loss of Eq.~\eqref{eq:loss} times $n$, which gives the penalty
weight $\lambda' = \lambda n / \sum_i v_i$. BCE is linear in $y$, so the expectation gives:
\[
f(r) = -\mu \log\sigma(b + r) - (1 - \mu)\log\bigl(1 - \sigma(b + r)\bigr) + \lambda' r^2 .
\]

\textbf{Need to prove:} $f'(r) = \sigma(b + r) - \mu + 2\lambda' r$.
\begin{align*}
f'(r) &= -\mu\bigl(1 - \sigma(b+r)\bigr) - (1-\mu)\bigl(-\sigma(b+r)\bigr) + 2\lambda' r && \text{given 2, chain rule}\\
      &= -\mu + \mu\sigma(b+r) + \sigma(b+r) - \mu\sigma(b+r) + 2\lambda' r && \text{distributive law}\\
      &= \sigma(b+r) - \mu + 2\lambda' r && \text{the $\mu\sigma$ terms cancel}
\end{align*}
\textbf{Then:}
\begin{align*}
f''(r) &= \sigma'(b+r) + 2\lambda' && \text{derivative of the line above}\\
       &> 0 && \text{given 1 and } \lambda' > 0
\end{align*}
\textbf{Therefore,} by given 3, $f$ is strictly convex and its minimiser $r^{*}$ is the only root of $f'$, so
$\sigma(b + r^{*}) + 2\lambda' r^{*} = \mu$.

\textbf{Choose:} $\hat r = \operatorname{logit}\mu - b$, the calibrated offset, so $\sigma(b + \hat r) = \mu$.

\textbf{Need to prove:} $0 < r^{*} < \hat r$ if $\hat r > 0$, $\hat r < r^{*} < 0$ if $\hat r < 0$, and
$r^{*} = 0$ if and only if $\hat r = 0$.
\begin{align*}
f'(0)      &= \sigma(b) - \mu = \sigma(b) - \sigma(b + \hat r) && \text{substitution of } \mu\\
f'(\hat r) &= \sigma(b + \hat r) - \mu + 2\lambda' \hat r = 2\lambda' \hat r && \text{substitution of } \mu
\end{align*}
\textbf{Case $\hat r > 0$:} $\sigma$ is increasing (given 1), so $f'(0) < 0$, and $f'(\hat r) = 2\lambda'\hat r > 0$.

\textbf{Precondition.} Given 4 needs $f'$ continuous: $f'$ is a sum of $\sigma$, a constant and a linear term,
each continuous, so it is.

\textbf{Therefore,} by given 4, $f'$ has a root in $(0, \hat r)$, and by uniqueness it is $r^{*}$: $0 < r^{*} < \hat r$.

\textbf{Same method, case $\hat r < 0$:} $f'(0) > 0$ and $f'(\hat r) < 0$, so $\hat r < r^{*} < 0$.

\textbf{Case $\hat r = 0$:} $f'(0) = 0$, so $r^{*} = 0$. In the other direction, $r^{*} = 0$ gives
$f'(0) = \sigma(b) - \mu = 0$, so $\sigma(b) = \mu$ and $\hat r = 0$.

\textbf{Therefore} (i) $r^{*} = 0$ exactly when $\sigma(b) = \mu$; (ii) $r^{*}$ has the sign of $\hat r$; (iii)
$|r^{*}| < |\hat r|$ when $\hat r \neq 0$. With the cap the reachable offsets are the interval $(-c, c)$. $f$ is
strictly convex, so on that interval it decreases toward $r^{*}$: the fitted offset is $r^{*}$ when $|r^{*}| < c$
and approaches the endpoint on the side of $r^{*}$ otherwise. The sign is kept and the size stays below $c$. \qed

\section{Settings and reproduction}
\label{app:settings}

Network for every recurrent arm: one GRU layer, hidden size \nNetHidden, cap \nNetCap{} (package default
\nDefCap), learning rate $\nNetLr$, weight decay \nNetWd, batch \nBatch, stride \nStride{} over the steps, one seed
and one bag. Epochs by size: PhysioNet and elec2 \nPhysDEpochs{} at \nSizeD{} and \nPhysMMEpochs{} at \nSizeMM;
synthetic \nSynCCLEpochs, \nSynMEpochs{} and \nSynMMMMEpochs{} at \nSizeCCL, \nSizeM{} and \nSizeMMMM. Penalty
sweep $\lambda \in \{0, 0.1, 0.3, 1, 3, 10\}$, chosen on an inner \nInnerTrainPct/\nInnerPct{} split. Trees:
learning rate \nGbmLr, \nGbmLeaves{} leaves, at most \nGbmMaxRounds{} rounds with early stopping after \nGbmStop{}
rounds on the inner holdout. The whole benchmark took \nWallTotalMin{} minutes on \nWorkers{} single-thread
workers. Code at commit \nCommit{} of the \texttt{anchorgru} repository; \texttt{bench/run\_all.sh} reruns the
benchmark, \texttt{results/make\_numbers.py} and \texttt{figures/make.py} regenerate every number and figure here.
