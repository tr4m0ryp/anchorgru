\begin{abstract}
A prior probability for a binary outcome often exists, and a recurrent network trained freely on each example's
history can end up worse than it. We add the network's output to the prior's log-odds as a bounded offset whose
output layer starts at zero, and penalise the squared offset itself, with a strength picked on an inner holdout. On a
synthetic task with a known answer (\nSizeM{} training and \nSynMNtest{} test rows), held-out log-loss falls by
\nSynMDabsfourPrior{} nats (SE \nSynMSEPrior) against the prior alone, \nSynMDabsfourLzero{} (SE \nSynMSELzero)
against the same model without the penalty and \nSynMDabsfourUnanch{} (SE \nSynMSEUnanch) against an unanchored
recurrent network, the one setting where it beats every arm by more than \nKeepSE{} SE. On ICU mortality it loses
to boosted trees started from the prior by \nPhysMMDabsfourGbm{} nats; under a boosted-tree prior it only ties the
prior, while the unpenalised model is \nPhysGbmMMDabsfourLzero{} nats worse. Differences are paired on fixed test
sets of \nPhysMMNtest{} to \nElecMMNtest{} rows over \nSplitCount{} training subsamples; standard errors are over
test examples.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem}
\label{sec:problem}

Many binary forecasting problems come with a prior probability $q_i$ before we train anything: a clinical severity
score, an upstream model, or a probability quoted by someone else. A sequence model that reads the example's recent
history can add information the prior misses. Trained without constraint, it can also do worse: it relearns what
the prior already encodes, overfits at small $n$, and nothing anchors it to the prior under distribution shift.

On ICU mortality with \nSizeD{} training stays, a recurrent network that gets the prior
as an input scores a held-out log-loss of \nPhysDUnanchLL{} nats; the prior alone scores \nPhysDPriorLL. The usual
fix is a residual on the prior's log-odds, as in residual policy learning
\citep{silver2018residual,johannink2019residual} and residual physics \citep{zeng2020tossingbot}. That alone is
insufficient: the same residual without a penalty scores \nPhysDLzeroLL, still worse than the prior. Weight decay does not bound
it either: Adam scales each step by the gradient's own size, so with decay \nNetWd{} the head still sits at its bound
on \nPhysDLzeroAtCap\% of training rows. We want a correction that stays near the prior unless the data argue
otherwise.

\section{The change}
\label{sec:change}

The model keeps the prior as an anchor on the log-odds scale ($\operatorname{logit} q = \log\frac{q}{1-q}$) and adds a bounded, gated offset from a gated recurrent
unit (GRU, a standard recurrent cell) \citep{cho2014gru}:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:model}
b_i = \operatorname{logit} q_i, \qquad
r_i = c \tanh\!\left(w^{\top} h_{i,T} + \beta\right) v_i, \qquad
p_i = \sigma\!\left(b_i + r_i\right),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:loss}
\mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} \operatorname{BCE}\!\left(y_i, p_i\right)
  + \lambda \, \frac{\sum_{i} r_i^{2}}{\sum_{i} v_i},
\qquad \operatorname{BCE}(y,p) = -y\log p - (1-y)\log(1-p).
\end{equation}
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{\hskip 1.5em}ll@{}}
\toprule
Symbol & Meaning & Symbol & Meaning \\
\midrule
$q_i$, $b_i$ & prior probability, its log-odds (clipped) & $v_i$ & validity gate, 0 or 1 \\
$h_{i,T}$ & GRU state after the last step & $c$ & bound on the offset (the cap) \\
$w$, $\beta$ & head weights and bias, start at 0 & $\lambda$ & penalty strength \\
$r_i$ & offset added to the prior's log-odds & $\sigma$ & logistic function \\
$p_i$ & predicted probability & $y_i$ & observed label, 0 or 1 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The GRU reads each value channel relative to its last observed value after a forward fill (carrying the last value
forward), plus one 0/1 observation-mask channel per value channel, which is the mask of GRU-D \citep{che2018grud}
without its decay. When fewer than a share \nMinObs{} of the steps are observed we set $v_i = 0$, and the example
gets the prior back. Because the head starts at zero, the untrained model equals the prior exactly, the ReZero idea
\citep{bachlechner2021rezero} applied to a probability. With $\lambda = 0$ our loss is the plain cross-entropy and
training matches an unpenalised loop bit for bit (tested in the package).

The prior enters as a fixed offset, as in a generalised linear model \citep{mccullagh1989glm}. The part we study
is the penalty. It acts on each example's offset $r_i$ and not on the weights, which is where weight decay and L2-SP
\citep{li2018l2sp} act. Penalising outputs instead of weights is known from function-space regularisation
\citep{benjamin2019function} and from distillation toward an earlier model \citep{li2018lwf}; here the reference is
an external prior and the penalty is the squared log-odds offset. Read as a maximum a posteriori fit (the single most probable parameter
setting), $\lambda r_i^2$ is a zero-mean Gaussian prior on the correction itself. The cap bounds the worst case; the
penalty determines how much evidence a correction requires before it departs from the prior. We pick $\lambda$ from \nSweepCount{} values
on an inner \nInnerPct\% holdout of the training rows (ties go to the larger value) and refit on all training
rows. Lemma~\ref{lem:shrink}
states what the penalty does at the population level (proof in Appendix~\ref{app:proof}); a step-by-step recipe
for new data is in Appendix~\ref{app:settings}.

\begin{lemma}[pointwise shrinkage]
\label{lem:shrink}
Fix one input with anchor $b$ and let $\mu = \Pr(y = 1 \mid \text{input}) \in (0,1)$. Let
$\lambda' = \lambda n / \sum_i v_i > 0$. The objective $f(r) = \mathbb{E}[\operatorname{BCE}(y, \sigma(b + r))] +
\lambda' r^2$ is strictly convex, and its minimiser $r^{*}$ solves $\sigma(b + r^{*}) + 2\lambda' r^{*} = \mu$.
(i) $r^{*} = 0$ if and only if the prior is calibrated at that input, $\sigma(b) = \mu$.
(ii) $r^{*}$ has the sign of $\operatorname{logit}\mu - b$.
(iii) $|r^{*}| < |\operatorname{logit}\mu - b|$ whenever $\sigma(b) \neq \mu$.
With the cap, the offset is also below $c$ in size.
\end{lemma}

In words, the penalised correction moves toward the calibrated one and stops short of it; a calibrated prior is
left alone. This is a population statement, not a promise that a fitted model never does worse than its prior.

\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Data.} We use three public tasks. None of their test sets is touched by training, tuning or the choice
of $\lambda$. \emph{Synthetic}: a logistic prior on static features plus \nSynChannels{} random-walk channels of
\nSynSteps{} steps with \nSynMissPct\% missing; the true probability is known, so the best reachable log-loss is
known too (\nSynBayesLL{} nats). \emph{elec2} \citep{harries1999elec2,gama2004drift}: does the electricity price
end above its daily average. Its prior is a logistic regression on the current row, refitted weekly on the previous
\nElecPriorRows{} rows, and the sequence is the last \nElecWindow{} half-hours of \nElecCols{} columns.
\emph{PhysioNet 2012} \citep{silva2012physionet,goldberger2000physiobank}: in-hospital death from an hourly grid of
\nPhysVars{} variables over \nPhysHours{} hours (\nPhysPosRate\% positive), with a logistic prior on the SAPS-I
admission score, a standard ICU severity score \citep{legall1984saps}. We fit that prior out of fold (each row scored by a fit that never saw it);
an in-sample prior teaches the residual to undo the prior's own overfit. Training sizes range from \nSizeCCL{} to
\nSizeMMMM{} rows.

\paragraph{Arms.} All arms share the same windows, rows and seed. \emph{Prior alone}. \emph{Penalty 0}: the anchored
model with $\lambda = 0$, that is, the method without our change, on the same code path. \emph{Unanchored GRU}: the same network started at the base rate, with no cap and no
penalty, and the prior's log-odds as an extra input channel. \emph{LightGBM from prior}: gradient-boosted trees
\citep{ke2017lightgbm} whose starting score is the prior's log-odds, stopped early on the same inner holdout, fed
per-variable summaries (PhysioNet) or the network's own flattened steps. \emph{Ours}: the anchored model with the
chosen $\lambda$. The fixed-$\lambda$ sweep and \nAblationCount{} ablations run alongside. Every recurrent arm uses
one small network: hidden size \nNetHidden, cap $c = \nNetCap$, AdamW (Adam with decoupled weight decay)
\citep{loshchilov2019adamw} at learning rate $\nNetLr$ and weight decay \nNetWd, one seed and one bootstrap
resample (Appendix~\ref{app:settings}). Tuning budget: ours picks $\lambda$ from \nSweepCount{} values and LightGBM
its number of rounds, both on the same inner holdout; the other arms get no search.

\paragraph{Metric and error.} We score held-out log-loss in nats, the mean negative log-probability of the true
label, $\ell_i = \operatorname{BCE}(y_i, p_i)$. Against each arm $a$ we report the paired difference and its
standard error (SE), $\bar d = \frac{1}{n_{\text{test}}} \sum_{i} (\ell_i^{\text{ours}} - \ell_i^{a})$ and
$\operatorname{SE} = s_d / \sqrt{n_{\text{test}}}$, where $s_d$ is the standard deviation of the per-example differences, each test example first averaged over the
\nSplitCount{} training subsamples. Negative favours ours. Rows of elec2 depend on their neighbours, so there the SE
comes from a moving-block bootstrap (resampling contiguous runs of rows) \citep{kunsch1989bootstrap} with one-week
blocks of \nElecBlock{} rows and \nBootDraws{} draws. We call a difference a win only beyond \nKeepSE{} SE, and a
tie inside one SE.

The penalty also steadies training. In \nSeedSpreadSeeds-seed reruns at the smallest $n$, the unpenalised head
saturates its cap on \nSynSpreadLzeroAtCap\% to \nElecSpreadLzeroAtCap\% of training rows. Moving $\lambda$ from 0

predicted and observed frequency) at PhysioNet $n = \nSizeD$ is \nPhysDLzeroECE{} for penalty 0 and
\nPhysDOursECE{} for ours (reliability diagram in Appendix~\ref{app:rel}).

A strong prior is the hardest test of no harm. With LightGBM on all \nPhysHours-hour summaries as the prior, ours
ties it (\nPhysGbmDDPrior, SE \nPhysGbmDSEPrior{} at \nSizeD; \nPhysGbmMMDPrior, SE \nPhysGbmMMSEPrior{} at
\nSizeMM), and penalty 0 trails ours by \nPhysGbmDDabsLzero{} and \nPhysGbmMMDabsLzero{} nats. No gain, and no harm.

\section{Limits}
\label{sec:limits}

The complete win holds in a single setting. On PhysioNet the boosted trees remain ahead, and none of the
\nVariantCount{} variants we ran to close that gap succeeded (Appendix~\ref{app:extra}). The most useful one,
\nWdMult{} times the weight decay, improves ours
(\nPhysWdMMOursLL{} at \nSizeMM) but helps the unanchored GRU more (\nPhysWdMMUnanchLL): cap and penalty hold the
model near a weak anchor. With a prior that cannot see the second day, ours is worse than the prior at
$n = \nSizeD$ (\nPhysStrongDDPrior, SE \nPhysStrongDSEPrior); nowhere else does it lose to its prior.

These variants are post hoc, chosen after the PhysioNet result. The arms, the \nSweepCount-value sweep, the
\nAblationCount{} ablations, the sizes and the keep rule were fixed before any run. One fix came after a run: the
first elec2 tree arm saw a shorter lag window (log-loss \nElecOldGbmLL); we report the rerun.

Our SE covers test-sample noise only. Taken over the \nSplitCount{} training subsamples, the SE of synthetic
$n = \nSizeM$ against penalty 0 grows from \nSynMSELzero{} to \nSynMSEsplitLzero, which puts that win on the
\nKeepSE{} SE boundary. Each arm is a single network rather than the package default of \nDefSeeds{} seeds by \nDefBags{}
bootstrap resamples. We dropped full-size training (compute), a GRU-D arm (library unavailable), an ablation without
the mask channels and a weather task (no data access). We claim no finite-sample guarantee, no advantage over boosted trees on tabular summaries, and no novelty
for the zero start or the cap.

\section{Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:shrink}}
\label{app:proof}

\textbf{Given.}
1) $\sigma(z) = 1/(1 + e^{-z})$ has $\sigma'(z) = \sigma(z)(1 - \sigma(z)) > 0$ for every real $z$.
2) $\frac{d}{dz}\log\sigma(z) = 1 - \sigma(z)$ and $\frac{d}{dz}\log(1 - \sigma(z)) = -\sigma(z)$.
3) A differentiable function with a strictly increasing derivative is strictly convex, and a point where that
derivative is zero is its unique minimiser.
4) Intermediate value theorem: a continuous $g$ with $g(a) < 0 < g(b)$ has a root strictly between $a$ and $b$.

\textbf{Choose:} $z = b + r$, and per example the loss of Eq.~\eqref{eq:loss} times $n$, which gives the penalty
weight $\lambda' = \lambda n / \sum_i v_i$. BCE is linear in $y$, so the expectation gives:
\[
f(r) = -\mu \log\sigma(b + r) - (1 - \mu)\log\bigl(1 - \sigma(b + r)\bigr) + \lambda' r^2 .
\]

\textbf{Need to prove:} $f'(r) = \sigma(b + r) - \mu + 2\lambda' r$.
\begin{align*}
f'(r) &= -\mu\bigl(1 - \sigma(b+r)\bigr) - (1-\mu)\bigl(-\sigma(b+r)\bigr) + 2\lambda' r && \text{given 2, chain rule}\\
      &= -\mu + \mu\sigma(b+r) + \sigma(b+r) - \mu\sigma(b+r) + 2\lambda' r && \text{distributive law}\\
      &= \sigma(b+r) - \mu + 2\lambda' r && \text{the $\mu\sigma$ terms cancel}
\end{align*}
\textbf{Then:}
\begin{align*}
f''(r) &= \sigma'(b+r) + 2\lambda' && \text{derivative of the line above}\\
       &> 0 && \text{given 1 and } \lambda' > 0
\end{align*}
\textbf{Therefore,} by given 3, $f$ is strictly convex and its minimiser $r^{*}$ is the only root of $f'$, so
$\sigma(b + r^{*}) + 2\lambda' r^{*} = \mu$.

\textbf{Choose:} $\hat r = \operatorname{logit}\mu - b$, the calibrated offset, so $\sigma(b + \hat r) = \mu$.

\textbf{Need to prove:} $0 < r^{*} < \hat r$ if $\hat r > 0$, $\hat r < r^{*} < 0$ if $\hat r < 0$, and
$r^{*} = 0$ if and only if $\hat r = 0$.
\begin{align*}
f'(0)      &= \sigma(b) - \mu = \sigma(b) - \sigma(b + \hat r) && \text{substitution of } \mu\\
f'(\hat r) &= \sigma(b + \hat r) - \mu + 2\lambda' \hat r = 2\lambda' \hat r && \text{substitution of } \mu
\end{align*}
\textbf{Case $\hat r > 0$:} $\sigma$ is increasing (given 1), so $f'(0) < 0$, and $f'(\hat r) = 2\lambda'\hat r > 0$.

\textbf{Precondition.} Given 4 needs $f'$ continuous: $f'$ is a sum of $\sigma$, a constant and a linear term,
each continuous, so it is.

\textbf{Therefore,} by given 4, $f'$ has a root in $(0, \hat r)$, and by uniqueness it is $r^{*}$: $0 < r^{*} < \hat r$.

\textbf{Same method, case $\hat r < 0$:} $f'(0) > 0$ and $f'(\hat r) < 0$, so $\hat r < r^{*} < 0$.

\textbf{Case $\hat r = 0$:} $f'(0) = 0$, so $r^{*} = 0$. In the other direction, $r^{*} = 0$ gives
$f'(0) = \sigma(b) - \mu = 0$, so $\sigma(b) = \mu$ and $\hat r = 0$.

\textbf{Therefore} (i) $r^{*} = 0$ exactly when $\sigma(b) = \mu$; (ii) $r^{*}$ has the sign of $\hat r$; (iii)
$|r^{*}| < |\hat r|$ when $\hat r \neq 0$. With the cap the reachable offsets are the interval $(-c, c)$. $f$ is
strictly convex, so on that interval it decreases toward $r^{*}$: the fitted offset is $r^{*}$ when $|r^{*}| < c$
and approaches the endpoint on the side of $r^{*}$ otherwise. The sign is kept and the size stays below $c$. \qed

\section{Settings and reproduction}
\label{app:settings}

\paragraph{Applying it to new data.} The method needs, per example, a prior probability $q_i$ computed without
the example's label (out of fold if the prior is fitted on the same rows), a label $y_i$, and a window of past
values of shape steps $\times$ channels with missing values marked. Steps: clip $q_i$ and take its log-odds; forward
fill each channel, express it relative to its last observed value and add the 0/1 mask channels; fit the model of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:model} with the loss of Eq.~\eqref{eq:loss} for each $\lambda$ in the grid on \nInnerTrainPct\% of the
training rows; keep the $\lambda$ with the lowest log-loss on the other \nInnerPct\%; refit on all training rows.
The package wraps this in a scikit-learn style classifier, \texttt{AnchoredGRUClassifier}, whose \texttt{fit} takes
windows of shape $(n, T, C)$ with NaN for missing values plus \texttt{prior=}$q$.

\paragraph{Benchmark settings.} Network for every recurrent arm: one GRU layer, hidden size \nNetHidden, cap \nNetCap{} (package default
\nDefCap), learning rate $\nNetLr$, weight decay \nNetWd, batch \nBatch, stride \nStride{} over the steps, one seed
and one bootstrap resample. Epochs by size: PhysioNet and elec2 \nPhysDEpochs{} at \nSizeD{} and \nPhysMMEpochs{} at \nSizeMM;
synthetic \nSynCCLEpochs, \nSynMEpochs{} and \nSynMMMMEpochs{} at \nSizeCCL, \nSizeM{} and \nSizeMMMM. Penalty
sweep $\lambda \in \{\nSweepList\}$, chosen on an inner \nInnerTrainPct/\nInnerPct{} split. Trees:
learning rate \nGbmLr, \nGbmLeaves{} leaves, at most \nGbmMaxRounds{} rounds with early stopping after \nGbmStop{}
rounds on the inner holdout. The whole benchmark took \nWallTotalMin{} minutes on \nWorkers{} single-thread
workers. Code at commit \nCommit{} of the \texttt{anchorgru} repository; \texttt{bench/run\_all.sh} reruns the
benchmark, \texttt{results/make\_numbers.py} and \texttt{figures/make.py} regenerate every number and figure here.
The change itself, the penalty term and the zero-started bounded head, is in \texttt{anchorgru/net/residual.py}; with $\lambda = 0$ that loss returns the plain cross-entropy, so penalty 0 is the unchanged baseline.
Dependencies as pinned in \texttt{pyproject.toml} (PyTorch \citep{paszke2019pytorch} $\geq$ 2.1, LightGBM $\geq$ 4.0).
